# Goal-alignment preflight

Use at a new workstream, material scope/design or resource change, important PR/release milestone, and repository-status snapshot. Reuse a still-applicable record; do not interview the user again merely because a workflow resumed. This preflight checks direction, not permission to execute.

## Bind work to the goal

Read the applicable human direction and accepted contract before judging implementation or tests. Retain a compact record:

- Objective, source, revision and exact approval scope; distinguish direct human evidence, reported approval and inferred intent.
- Measurable user/product/research success criteria, separately from technical ACs and delivery checks.
- Non-goals and responsibility boundaries.
- Resource envelope: known time, cost, compute, maintenance and allocation constraints; mark unspecified limits `unknown`, without inventing a budget.
- Actual proposed/completed work and the material delta since the last aligned snapshot.
- Alignment: `aligned|off-goal|unknown`; evidence, outcome still unverified, and next action within current authority.

Trace each material addition to a goal or prerequisite. Consider cumulative cost and scope across the bounded workstream, not just whether each small patch is defensible. Green CI, a runnable research pipeline, a merged PR or a successful release proves only its named technical/delivery condition; it cannot establish user acceptance, hypothesis validity or scientific PASS.

## Preserve ownership and current direction

Human authority owns purpose, direction, priority, resource allocation, material architecture/product design and critical policy/risk acceptance. Arca owns analysis, option comparison, proactive detection and independent verification. OpenCode owns authorized implementation, tests and noncritical technical choices. An accepted contract can resolve a choice without another Ask.

Apply a later explicit human goal/priority change to its stated scope. Preserve unaffected grants and constraints; do not turn a recommendation into the new roadmap or a scoped reprioritization into permission for unrelated expansion. Keep the superseded source and the reason/time of supersession.

When alignment is unknown, inspect the smallest available authoritative source first. Missing documentation alone does not prove drift or require a new approval. If the unknown controls a consequential next action, hold only that action and explain the unresolved choice. Continue separable authorized work.

When work is off-goal or exceeds the known envelope, expose the affected human-owned choice through `approval-brief`. Compare a lower-cost/smaller scope and stopping or retaining the current state; recommend one with evidence. Do not silently revise the goal, expand the budget, or undo completed work. Record the result through the caller's existing checkpoint owner; this reference creates no writer, queue or execution authority.
